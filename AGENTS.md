# UMTO mission authoring

For every future UMTO and Little Panth mission, plan a purposeful shot list before implementing the story. Use the shared cinematic camera described in [missions/MISSION_FORMAT.md](missions/MISSION_FORMAT.md). Establish locations with wides, frame dialogue with medium or two-shots, use reaction/insert close-ups for important beats, track meaningful movement, and use restrained pan/dolly/parallax when the scene benefits. High/low/overhead/POV/OTS require accurate viewpoint art or a genuine 3D scene; never distort flat sprites to fake perspective. Use Dutch angle only for tension. Keep interactive targets visible in play mode and keep captions separate from camera transforms. Honor reduced motion, keep spiritual/educational moments calm, and ensure the same shot list works in watch mode and export. Test camera math, story flows, and mobile/browser presentation before claiming deployment is verified.

For new Little Panth animated storybooks, target 170–200 spoken Punjabi words. Mission 05, The Thirsty Crow, is the 186-word reference. Count the narration and character dialogue in scene order.

## Little Panth visual and catalogue standards

Keep animal characters modestly scaled in the scene; use smooth organic silhouettes and curved, deforming limbs, not rigid toy-like segments. Use recognizable species anatomy. Leave clear negative space between faces and antennae in every dialogue two-shot, entrance, crossing and shared-action pose. Review intermediate frames as well as endpoints on desktop and mobile; use shot changes or reposition actors to avoid head/limb overlap. Preserve the established picture-tap playback, clean final frame, Gemini Punjabi recordings and aligned Gurmukhi highlighting; do not add replay buttons. Update both Little Panth contents pages with source attribution, Aesop-related counts (distinguish shared and inspired versions) and approximate duration. Use recorded speech plus scene padding when available, mark script-based estimates explicitly.

A traditional loose chunni must read as one continuous long dupatta: rest it over the head and shoulders, drape it naturally across the chest, and let broad unequal ends fall along the kameez toward the knees. Use the shared UMTO headwear and garment renderer so existing stories and future characters inherit improvements. Check the whole outfit and moving shoulder joins; a short cape or detached neck scarf does not represent this dress. Keep wrapped shawls over the upper arms, with hands emerging at a real fabric edge. Animal walking must retain planted foot contact with the ground or a visibly supporting surface throughout the action.

For each new Little Panth episode, preserve the explicit `?compilation=1` full-episode playback API (`ready`, `start`, `status`) and the selection checkboxes, source and duration data on both contents pages. Test the selected-episode export with real recorded audio, and do not claim an MP4 was produced until rendering succeeds.

The production tracker is owner-approved only: new stories start unchecked/unapproved, checked is required before approval, only approved episodes are eligible for named compilations, and successful render history is never inferred from a selection or a test render. If an approved story is substantially revised, flag it for re-review. Keep production-db.json as the durable shared tracker and use the authenticated workflow for updates; never expose GitHub credentials in the site.

## Shared character acting standard (v108 onward)

Use the shared UMTO character rig for future human characters and use the new emotional reaction and explanation animation library wherever the story beat calls for it. Choose poses and expressions intentionally in the shot list: use calm, curious, concerned, delighted, relieved, skeptical, embarrassed, frightened or astonished reactions as appropriate; use animated anticipation, a readable hold and a natural recovery rather than a frozen face. Keep exaggeration playful and age-appropriate, and restrained in devotional scenes.

Vary spoken dialogue gestures: prefer a single open palm for ordinary explanation, alternate hands between distinct ideas, reserve pointing for emphasis, use two hands for comparisons, and use a small presentation or hand-to-chest gesture for sincere moments. Do not repeat the same two-arm loop throughout a story. Use floor sitting and kneeling for appropriate sangat, classroom, seva and domestic beats; ensure both hands are visibly in front when resting on the lap or knees, both legs read as folded, and long fabric follows the pose without intersecting the limbs. Use barefoot art where religious context requires it. Front-facing walks need visible alternating depth, relaxed counter-swing, stable planted soles and appropriate pacing. Review front and both three-quarter views, start/mid/end frames, clothing overlaps and mobile framing. Keep prop grips and lip-sync authoritative over decorative gestures.

For new native-rig stories, use explicit `pose.expression` and `pose.gesture` selections when supported by the player, and choose the posture appropriate to the scene. Do not assume an old story automatically has new authored poses. Verify each new story's animation and export with actual rendered frames before approving it.
