# Mission 35 — Belling the Cat / ਬਿੱਲੀ ਦੇ ਗਲ ਘੰਟੀ

## Story and source

An original Punjabi retelling of the fable in *The Æsop for Children* (1919),
as presented by the Library of Congress: https://www.read.gov/aesop/003.html.
Keep the classic question: a bell could warn the mice, but who can attach it?
The cat never actually wears the bell. No mouse is caught or hurt.
Target 170–200 spoken Punjabi words, with a warm narrator, a bright young mouse,
a thoughtful older mouse and a third mouse reacting to the proposal.

## Visual direction

A warm Punjabi grain storeroom: ochre plaster, a few exposed bricks, woven grain
sacks, a low wooden shelf, scattered wheat and a small mouse doorway. A broad
open doorway on the right shows a sunlit courtyard. Leave the central floor
clear for the council and a small brass bell. Reuse the improved mouse artwork
from Mission 006 and gray tabby cat artwork from Mission 032. Use smooth curved
limbs and grounded walking with at least one visible planted supporting paw.
The three mice have distinct coat tones and clear face/ear separation. Keep the
cat naturally larger than the mice. Treat gestures as forepaw movements while
the remaining paws support the body. A nudge may rock the bell against the floor;
the bell is never suspended without a visible grip or support.
